Disentangling Spurious Correlations: How IRM Handles Diverse Shortcuts in Text Classification#

Authors: Paul Grunenwald, Benoît Crabbé, Guillaume Wisniewski, Pirmin Lemberger Venue: arXiv (2026)

In NLP, models trained via Empirical Risk Minimization (ERM) often exploit spurious correlations, such as dialectal markers, at the expense of robust linguistic features, thereby degrading their out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization. Invariant Risk Minimization (IRM) aims to suppress such shortcuts by identifying predictive relationships that remain stable across environments, but its practical utility for NLP remains debated. We use a causality-driven framework to evaluate IRM under three shortcut-generating mechanisms: label-generated features, confounding, and selection bias. Our results show that IRM successfully rejects shortcuts across the evaluated causal and anti-causal structures, including under limited environmental variation and increasing label noise. We then extend our analysis beyond controlled synthetic artifacts to two less constrained settings: gender-profession associations in Bias in Bios and semantically loaded actor-name shortcuts in ActorCorr. These experiments show that invariant learning can mitigate more realistic textual shortcuts, while semantically meaningful features require careful calibration of the invariance penalty.

Mind the Accuracy-Approval Gap: How Users Evaluate Sycophantic AI in Everyday Tasks#

Authors: Wajiha Naveed, Brandon Fain Venue: arXiv (2026)

Large language models (LLMs) often exhibit sycophancy, a tendency to agree with users, flatter or affirm them, and preserve their face, even when independent judgment or critical feedback would be more useful. Prior work has largely evaluated sycophancy through benchmark evaluations, but less is known about how users perceive and respond to sycophantic AI in everyday tasks. We combine benchmark evaluations with two large-scale user studies. We study four personas defined through system prompts: objective (instructed to prioritize accuracy over agreement), soft-sycophantic, hard-sycophantic, and baseline (no persona-specific system prompt). Benchmarks show that even baseline exhibits substantial sycophancy, agreeing with users’ stated views 71.7% of the time on NLP Survey, compared with 54.8% for objective and 89.5% for hard-sycophantic. Under conversational pressure on TruthfulQA, accuracy ranged from 81.9% for objective to 59.6% for baseline and 8.7% for hard-sycophantic. Participants were assigned to one of the four personas, and either discussed contested policy topics or collaborated with AI on email writing. Despite its higher accuracy under pressure, objective AI was rated significantly less respectful and satisfying than the other personas, and less trustworthy than baseline. Baseline and sycophantic personas did not differ significantly. Persona did not significantly affect how often participants changed their opinions, although participants who discussed with objective AI had greater odds of reduced confidence. Objective AI prompted greater writing expansion than soft-sycophantic AI, but final writing quality did not differ significantly across personas. These findings highlight a tension between accuracy and user approval: personas with substantially lower accuracy under conversational pressure received more favorable ratings for respect and satisfaction than objective AI.

Reducing Batch Makespan for LLM Agent Workloads through Inference-Environment Coordination#

Authors: Wei Feng, Meiling Chen, Boxiao Chen, Lu Liu, Bin Zi, Libin Ye, Xingyu Lu, Lei Xu, Jun Zhou Venue: arXiv (2026)

Efficient batch execution of agent tasks is critical to LLM training and development workflows, including benchmark evaluation, synthetic-data generation, and RL rollouts. Since these tasks alternate between LLM inference and environment interaction, we present coordinated optimizations to reduce batch makespan, the time required to complete all tasks in a batch. For hybrid LLMs, a workload-based resource model guides GPU-memory allocation between key-value (KV) cache and recurrent state and provides a reference sample concurrency to exploit parallelism while limiting recomputation. Trajectory-aware routing and scheduling promote cache reuse while balancing worker load. Under constrained environment capacity, we decouple sample admission from environment execution and introduce Chain-Advancing Action Scheduling (CAAS), which prioritizes long chains using observed progress to shorten the batch tail. Controlled replay combines actual inference with recorded environment responses and durations for ablations of individual optimizations. Live evaluations with Ling-3.0-Flash and Ling-3.1-Flash on the BFCL, SWE-bench Multilingual, WideSearch, and MCP-Atlas benchmarks demonstrate makespan reductions of 10.4% to 65.0%, while evaluation scores remain aligned. Furthermore, these methods have been successfully applied to training and evaluation workflows for the Ling-3.x model series, accelerating the model-development cycle.

Unibuc - NLP at “Voight-Kampff” Generative AI Detection PAN 2025#

Authors: Teodor Marchitan, Claudiu Creanga, Liviu P. Dinu Venue: arXiv (2026)

This paper describes the approach of the Unibuc - NLP team in tackling the “Voight-Kampff” Generative AI Detection task from PAN at CLEF 2025. In the Subtask 1: Voight-Kampff AI Detection Sensitivity we have explored different approaches of leveraging embeddings extracted from LLMs, using either classical ML models or further fine-tuning LLMs with a classification head for the downstream binary classification task. Our experiments showed that using a majority vote ensemble of classical ML models (Light GBM, XGB, Logistic Regression and SVM) is comparable with fine-tuning for downstream task achieving around 99.4% F1 score. For the Subtask 2: Human-AI Collaborative Text Classification we explored a way of combining features at different layers extracted using transformer backbone with layer-wise projection and attentive pooling which proved modest results.

IsharaBART: An efficient BART-Based Framework for Low-Resource Bangla Sign Language Translation#

Authors: Aonmoy Das, Ananna Dev Aishi, Md. Fazlul Kader Venue: arXiv (2026)

Millions of Bangla Sign Language (BdSL) users lack reliable automatic translation tools. Existing systems are limited by data scarcity, unidirectional design, and computationally expensive architectures that are not suitable for low-resource deployment. We introduce IsharaBART, a bidirectional generative framework for gloss-sentence translation in BdSL. IsharaBART uses Bidirectional and Auto-Regressive Transformers (BART)-based conditional language modeling with denoising learning objectives. We developed a parallel dataset of 1,000 professionally annotated gloss-sentence pairs, balanced across tenses and structures. We expanded the dataset to 4,831 pairs through four augmentation techniques: rule-based tense transformations, masked token substitutions, back-translation, and one-shot prompting. A compact denoising sequence-to-sequence model was pre-trained from scratch on monolingual Bangla text from six different corpora, then fine-tuned on the annotated and augmented datasets. We compare IsharaBART against four larger baseline models under identical conditions, where it achieves comparable or superior bidirectional performance. On the gloss-to-sentence (G2S) task, it attains Bilingual Evaluation Understudy (BLEU-4) score up to 51.7, and on the sentence-to-gloss (S2G) task, it achieves 61.40, outperforming multilingual models despite having fewer than 10% of the parameters. Ablation studies demonstrate that pre-training provides 62.5-90.4% faster convergence and 11.5-118% higher BLEU-4 than scratch. Low-rank adaptation (LoRA) reduces trainable parameters by 99.3%, but with 28.0-52.2% loss of BLEU-4 compared to full-parameter fine-tuning.

When does text encoding matter for language modeling?#

Authors: Eugene Jang, Catherine Arnett, Terra Blevins Venue: arXiv (2026)

UTF-8’s role as the de facto encoding standard in language models introduces cross-lingual inequities due to its variable-length design. However, the effect that this encoding choice has on model performance remains largely unexamined. In this work, we investigate how text encoding impacts the performance of byte-level language models, comparing UTF-8 with a wide range of other encoding strategies, including other Unicode-based approaches, language-specific encodings, and our own customized encodings. To facilitate our analysis, we propose Aether: a hybrid encoding scheme that uses script-specific encodings for our target setting while maintaining full Unicode coverage through UTF-8 fallbacks, using this framework to model four orthographically diverse languages. Surprisingly, we find that while languages with information-dense characters (Chinese, Korean) significantly benefit from compressive encodings, languages whose characters carry less information individually (Georgian, Tamil) are largely insensitive to encoding choice. In light of these findings, we argue that text encoding should be a language-specific design decision, rather than a universal default.

BDBBQ: A Human-AI Collaborative Benchmark for Bengali Social Bias in LLMs#

Authors: Provat Saha, Ashika Habib Khan, Tahseen Tanha, Hasin Ishrak, Muhammad Iqbal Hossain Venue: arXiv (2026)

Despite growing attention to social bias in large language models (LLMs), existing bias benchmarks provide limited coverage of culturally grounded evaluation in Bengali, particularly within Bangladesh’s sociocultural context. To address this gap, we develop a bilingual extension of BanglaBBQ comprising 61,312 question-answering instances across 12 social categories in English and Bengali. The benchmark incorporates balanced answer permutations, contrasting question framings, and both ambiguous and disambiguated contexts to systematically examine model performance and stereotype-aligned bias across languages. We validate the dataset through structural checks, 220 statistical tests, and expert annotation of 150 instances by four annotators, achieving strong inter-annotator agreement (Fleiss’ \k{appa}=0.8349). We evaluate eight LLMs across 490,496 instances and identify five key findings. First, disambiguated contexts yield substantially greater accuracy improvements in Bengali (+36.59 percentage points) than in English (+17.71). Second, all evaluated models exhibit lower accuracy in Bengali, although Mistral-Nemo shows the smallest cross-lingual accuracy gap (5.12 percentage points). Third, stereotype-aligned bias exhibits language and model dependent variation, increasing in Bengali for Gemma 2 9B but decreasing for Mistral-Nemo. Fourth, higher accuracy does not necessarily correspond to lower stereotype-aligned bias. Finally, bias varies considerably across social categories, with Qwen 2.5 7B exhibiting stronger stereotype alignment in Bengali for Politics and Physical Appearance. These findings demonstrate that cross-lingual performance and social bias are distinct dimensions of LLM behavior and underscore the importance of culturally grounded, language-sensitive benchmarks for evaluating fairness beyond English-centric settings.

Discriminator-free adversarial domain adaptation for robust speech classification#

Authors: Yacouba Kaloga, Ina Kodrasi Venue: arXiv (2026)

The performance of speech classification systems such as speech emotion recognition or pathological speech detection can degrade substantially when there is a domain shift between training and test data. This shift can arise due to e.g., differences in recording setup, acoustic environment, speaker, language, or speaking style. Adversarial domain adaptation addresses this problem by encouraging representations that are less sensitive to domain-specific differences. However, many adversarial approaches rely on training a separate domain discriminator, which can be unstable and difficult to train. In this paper, we propose to use a discriminator-free adversarial learning network (DALN) for unsupervised domain adaptation in speech classification tasks. DALN reuses the task classifier as an implicit discriminator and aligns domains through the nuclear-norm Wasserstein discrepancy on classifier outputs. We extensively evaluate the performance of DALN on intra-lingual, cross-lingual, and noisy adaptations for speech emotion recognition and pathological speech detection. Extensive experimental results show that DALN generally achieves the best performance across the considered tasks and domain shifts, outperforming discriminator-based domain adaptation approaches.

A Large-Scale Dataset for Cross-Lingual Multi-Jurisdiction Patent Drafting from Patent Cooperation Treaty Applications#

Authors: Trung Phuong Le, Hirofumi Nonaka, Koji Marusaki, Katsuhito Sudoh, Seiya Kawano Venue: arXiv (2026)

Securing patent protection across countries requires drafting claims separately for each national patent office, in the relevant language and according to its legal and stylistic conventions. Patent claims can therefore differ across languages and jurisdictions because of translation, target-office drafting practices, and prosecution-stage changes. We formulate cross-lingual patent drafting as generating plausible candidate claim sets for a specified target language, jurisdiction, and prosecution stage from a source claim set in another language. The task models jurisdiction- and stage-specific transformations beyond content-preserving translation. We construct a large-scale Japanese-English dataset by linking Japan Patent Office (JPO) and United States Patent and Trademark Office (USPTO) records through 884,179 Patent Cooperation Treaty (PCT)-derived patent families. Across six released configurations, these families yield 4,067,168 directional claim-set instances; four core configurations form the main benchmark. Commercial machine translation and zero- and few-shot prompting capture part of the task, while supervised adaptation provides the strongest overall baseline, showing that task-specific patterns can be learned from linked cross-jurisdictional claim sets.

Temporal Context-Aware Spam Detection Using Transformer-Based NLP Models#

Authors: Fariz Zeynalov, Razim Mammadli Venue: arXiv (2026)

Spam detection is usually treated as single-message classification, ignoring behavioral patterns that emerge across sequences of messages from the same sender. We study outbound SMS spam detection at the carrier level, where the goal is to predict at send time whether a carrier will flag a message as spam. Using a proprietary production database of 43,741 outbound SMS messages with UTC transmission timestamps and carrier delivery statuses, we report three sets of results. On an 8,749-message held-out test set, a TF-IDF + logistic regression baseline reaches AUC-ROC 0.8721, and a text-only DeBERTa-v3-base classifier reaches AUC-ROC 0.9011 and F1 0.8412. A deployed DistilBERT model augmented with inter-message temporal features reaches accuracy 0.8238, spam-class F1 0.8382, and recall 0.9128 at threshold 0.5 on a separate, class-balanced 20,000-message production evaluation set; these results are not directly comparable with the held-out test results. We also describe TemporalBertSpam, an architecture that encodes each sender’s message history with a causal Transformer, and outline the same-split evaluation needed to measure its contribution.

ALMeD: Medieval French Gold-Standard#

Authors: Ragini Menon, Sabine Tittel Venue: arXiv (2026)

Medieval French is one of the Romance vernacular languages with a significant history of transmission and an enormous influence on other vernacular languages of the time. The sources convey valuable knowledge about all aspects of culture. The study of Medieval French has, over the last few years, increasingly benefited from NLP-based methods. There have been numerous efforts to parse medieval French; however, these efforts use corpora comprising legal texts and – primarily – literary texts as their foundation and training data. This means that, except for juridical resources, scientific literature is overlooked. We present ALMeD, an annotated, semantically rich corpus (work-in-progress) of medical and surgical treatises produced in medieval French, i.e., Old and Middle French, including Anglo-Norman. Our semantically disambiguated gold standard goes beyond existing resources, focusing on medical terminology, and accounts for morphological development, evolving syntactical structures and the lack of spelling normalisation in these languages.

Persistent-mediator architecture for universal fault-tolerant quantum computation with color codes#

Authors: Zhi-Cheng He, Jingbo Wang, Dong E. Liu Venue: arXiv (2026)

Two-dimensional color codes provide transversal Clifford gates, whereas suitable three-dimensional codes provide a transversal $T$ gate. Representative cross-dimensional schemes either switch the computational encoding or consume a 3D encoded state. Reusing the same protected 3D block would remove repeated auxiliary-state preparation and terminal readout, raising a central question: can the 3D module remain encoded and protected while mediating successive $T$ gates on a separate 2D register? Here we introduce a cross-dimensional persistent-mediator architecture that retains the 2D data register and 3D mediator as distinct encoded modules at each cycle boundary, coupled only through a transient protected subsystem. We construct a membrane-like joint-logical measurement and combine it with a matched cross-dimensional $CZ$, complementary string-like surgery, and feed-forward to close a deterministic logical $T$-gate cycle. The cycle avoids post-selection and destructive mediator readout and restores the 3D block to its initial logical state. Stabilizer analysis establishes the logical action and preserved spatial distance. Phenomenological and circuit-level simulations of a Clifford surrogate show finite-distance scaling close to the matched 3D memory benchmark, while sequential tests show approximately linear logical-error accumulation in the low-error regime. Persistent mediation enables repeated access to higher-dimensional non-Clifford capability while keeping the computational register in its 2D encoding.

QVS-LIVO RiskMap: Health- and Information-Aware View Selection for Multi-Camera LIVO and Off-Road Risk Mapping#

Authors: Zihan Pan, Jinghan Wang, Hang Li, Feng Cheng, Tianchen Liu, Gaoliang Peng Venue: arXiv (2026)

Multi-camera observations in off-road environments vary significantly in reliability, posing challenges when complementing degraded geometric constraints. Motivated by this, we propose Quality-aware View Selection Lidar-Vision Odometry Risk Mapping (QVS-LIVO RiskMap), which decouples visual processing for state estimation from that for semantic mapping. The front end evaluates camera observations through read-only pretracking on a shared lidar-inertial posterior, combining observation health with geometric complementarity. Channel health memory and switching hysteresis govern selection of at most one eligible view for direct photometric correction. The posterior is retained when no view is eligible or the correction fails acceptance. The mapping back end integrates semantic observations from all available views in a shared world grid. Vehicle-scale geometry constrains semantic risk corrections, while spatial and temporal evidence supports risk confirmation. The system also produces a local bird’s-eye-view (BEV) risk map and a global 2.5D semantic-terrain risk map with obstacle instance voxels. On two field sequences, QVS-LIVO achieved horizontal root mean square errors of 0.910 m and 1.664 m. It reduced error by 43.3% relative to FAST-LIO2 on the degraded long-route sequence. Across 12 controlled camera-fault configurations involving dropout, black frames and 60 ms timestamp shifts, it maintained complete trajectories with errors of 1.54-2.23 m. Stellar-Field-Driven Induction Heating Unlikely To Cause Significant Heating in Giant Exoplanets#

Authors: Madison Brady, Darryl Z. Seligman, Cassidy Walker Venue: arXiv (2026)

A planet moving through a changing magnetic field will have a current induced on it by that magnetic field. If the magnetic Reynolds number in the upper atmosphere of this planet is small, the dissipation of this current could result in observable changes to the planet’s properties. In this paper, we estimated the total power that would be dissipated in the observed giant exoplanet population if they orbited stars with misaligned magnetic dipoles. We found that, even if all planets orbit stars with extremely misaligned surface magnetic fields, the vast majority of known exoplanets are unlikely to dissipate enough magnetic power to cause observable changes in their surface temperatures. We also extended our calculations to estimate the impact that a low-but-nonzero planetary eccentricity would have on the induced power, and provide a code that implements these calculations. Overall, while it is unlikely that any currently known exoplanets are experiencing a high enough degree of stellar-induced Ohmic heating to be observable, we cannot rule out the possibility that an eccentric, inclined planet on a short-period orbit around a superlatively magnetically active star could have observable magnetic heating. We note that our calculations assume that magnetic diffusion dominates over induction in the upper atmospheres of these planets. However, hot Jupiters with large wind speeds may have high magnetic Reynolds numbers and therefore be in the inductive regime.

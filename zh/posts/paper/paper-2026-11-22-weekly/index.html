Hybrid Retrieval for Government Scheme Search in English and Marathi#

Authors: Aishwarya Gunjal, Kanchan Khemnar, Pawan Poojary, Pranav Kulkarni, Raviraj Joshi, Manish Khodaskar Venue: arXiv (2026)

Retrieving government schemes requires matching citizens’ information needs to formal administrative descriptions, often across languages and terminology. We present a bilingual search system for Maharashtra government schemes and public services that combines BM25 retrieval with sentence-embedding similarity through equal-weight score fusion. The system indexes aligned English-Marathi scheme descriptions and supports multilingual, Marathi-focused, and translation-mediated semantic configurations. Evaluation on manually curated bilingual queries compares BM25, dense retrieval, and hybrid retrieval using IndicSBERT and MahaSBERT. BM25 provides a strong baseline with a mean reciprocal rank (MRR) of 0.864. Hybrid retrieval increases MRR to 0.872 with IndicSBERT and 0.879 with MahaSBERT, whereas the corresponding dense-only systems achieve 0.663 and 0.655. MahaSBERT-based fusion also increases precision from 0.781 to 0.808 while matching BM25 recall at 0.946. These results support the complementary use of lexical and semantic signals in this collection, while showing that improvements over a strong lexical baseline are modest and metric-dependent. The study provides an application-focused analysis of hybrid retrieval for bilingual public-service information access.

Language-Oriented Semantic Modeling for Target Language Extraction#

Authors: Mehmet Sinan Yıldırım, Ke Zhang, Shuai Wang, Haizhou Li Venue: arXiv (2026)

Target Language Extraction (TLE) aims to isolate speech in a target language from multilingual mixtures. Existing approaches primarily rely on acoustic modeling with language identity labels, which provide only coarse categorical guidance. In this work, we propose a language-oriented semantic modeling framework that augments acoustic extraction with language-specific semantic representations. A pre-trained speech semantic model is adapted via language-aware prompt conditioning and parameter-efficient fine-tuning to generate target-language-focused semantic representations from the mixture. {These capture high-level semantic structure of the acoustics and they} are integrated into the extraction model to provide structured semantic guidance beyond identity labels. Experiments on the CommonVoiceMix dataset show that incorporating semantic representations consistently improves over acoustic-only baselines, achieving up to 1.16 dB SI-SDR gain.

Web search in Latin America: language models use less local and diverse news than search engines#

Authors: Francisco W. Kerche, Franziska Sofia Hafner, Felix M. Simon, Luc Rocher Venue: arXiv (2026)

AI platforms are becoming a major gateway to news and current events. This shift risks amplifying existing geographical biases that favour narratives from wealthier countries over those from the Global South. This is particularly relevant in Latin America, which accounts for the largest share of AI usage in the Global South. We examine how information about Latin America changes when users shift from conventional search engines to LLM-based search. We construct a dataset of 1,901 current events across nine Latin American countries, craft realistic queries, and submit them to three search engines and four language models. We vary query language and user location to represent local, diaspora, tourist, and foreign user scenarios. Across 49,207 requests and 8,834 unique domains, language models return a less diverse set of news sources than search engines. Across user types, the share of domestic news returned by language models is inconsistent. For local users, while search engines already return only 41.3% local news, language models return a further 3.6 to 11.6 percentage points less. Taken together, our findings suggest that LLM-based search can reproduce and, in some settings, amplify geographical hierarchies in the reach and visibility of news sources. We release an open dataset to support multilingual and geographically diverse evaluation of LLM-based search, and highlight implications for more equitable information systems design.

When Should a Voice Agent Speak? End-of-Turn Detection in Turkish#

Authors: Ahmet Tuğrul Bayrak, Fatma Nur Korkmaz, Mustafa Sertaç Türkel, Nur Yağmur Tuncer Venue: arXiv (2026)

A voice agent that waits for a fixed silence before replying either interrupts users who pause mid-utterance or responds late to users who have finished. This study examines the decision at the onset of silence, using turn ends and turn-internal pauses extracted from the Real-TurnTurk corpus of Turkish dyadic video calls. A fine-tuned Turkish TurnGPT model, a zero-shot Turkish language model and a deployed multilingual detector are compared, together with voice-activity timing and facial features, under recording-disjoint and speaker-disjoint evaluation with paired cluster bootstrap tests. On held-out recordings, the fine-tuned model separates turn ends from pauses better than both text baselines and is the best calibrated of the three. Timing features of the current speaker are similarly informative, and their combination with the fine-tuned text score gives the best timing-based results; with logistic regression, this combination also outperforms the same combination with either text baseline. In a latency simulation, adding the text score to a silence timeout shortens the mean response delay at the same interruption rate. On automatic Whisper transcripts, the fine-tuned model retains its advantage over the zero-shot model.

PAIR-MED: Policy-reward Alternation for Iterative Refinement in Multilingual Medical Reasoning#

Authors: Fan Gao, Xiaohang Xu, Lang Huang, Nan Lu, Irene Li Venue: arXiv (2026)

Large language models demonstrate strong medical question-answering capabilities, yet reasoning performance remains uneven across languages. Process reward models (PRMs) provide step-level feedback for improving reasoning, but a fixed PRM may become increasingly misaligned with the trajectory distribution of an evolving policy during reinforcement learning, thereby reducing the reliability of its supervision. We propose a policy–reward alternating training framework that jointly adapts a medical PRM and optimizes a multilingual reasoning policy. Following supervised initialization, the current policy generates multilingual trajectories to update the PRM, which then provides process-level rewards for the next policy update. Medical step supervision maintains the PRM’s medical foundation, while process, outcome, and language-related rewards guide policy learning. We develop multilingual medical PRMs (PAIR-PRM) and reasoning models (PAIR-MED) at 8B and 9B scales across 12 languages. On the medical subsets of Global-MMLU and MMLU-ProX, our models improve average accuracy by 3.01–7.47% over their corresponding Qwen backbones. On CUREMED-BENCH, PAIR-MED-9B improves the joint language consistency and logical accuracy metric by 12.88% and 7.45% over baselines for in-domain and out-of-domain languages. Further analyses examine PRM adaptation, multilingual reasoning quality, and the contributions of individual training components.

Whose Safety? Addressing Anti-Blackness in the Age of AI Safety#

Authors: Mírian Silva, Serena Oduro Venue: arXiv (2026)

“AI safety” has grown from a niche contingent of actors into the dominant framing for artificial intelligence (AI) assessment and evaluation, overshadowing AI ethics. Through political and monetary influence, AI safety has shifted political focus within the US and abroad. The focus has shifted away from harms that currently affect the public toward threats framed as “existential risks”. We argue that safety cannot be achieved while anti-Blackness is left unaddressed. We ground this argument in Sylvia Wynter’s critique of the “overrepresentation” of Man: the modern, Western conception of the Human that is predicated on the subjugation and othering of Blackness. To analyze the state of addressing anti-Blackness in the age of AI safety, we conduct a two-stage, language model-assisted content analysis of 69,290 accepted main-track papers from ten leading machine learning (ML), natural language processing (NLP), and AI ethics conferences (2020–2025). Safety research grew from 0.5% of accepted papers in 2020 to 6.4% in 2025, overtaking ethics. Yet 94% of safety papers rely on automated metrics, and only 11% of safety papers mention harms that affect society directly, compared with 78% of ethics papers. Of the 2,182 safety papers, 8 mention race; none make it central, and none name Black or African-diaspora communities. Across all 254 identified as race-focused papers, only 3 describe racial harm in safety terms, and 55% appear at three ethics venues that publish 2.5% of the corpus. We offer this interdisciplinary examination of AI safety to argue that a just technological future depends on confronting anti-Blackness directly.

MoL-SQL: A Controlled Benchmark for Mixture-of-Language Text-to-SQL#

Authors: Tianqi Luo, Boyan Li, Yuyu Luo Venue: arXiv (2026)

Text-to-SQL systems are usually built and evaluated under a monolingual assumption, yet in international applications users often query databases stored in a different language. Existing multilingual benchmarks mostly translate only the question without aligning schema and value entities, so they can neither locate the source of an accuracy drop nor rule out translation errors. Hence, we introduce MoL-SQL, a benchmark for mixture-of-language Text-to-SQL that switches the languages of the question, schema, and stored values independently while keeping each task fixed, and grades every cross-lingual entity mapping into four difficulty levels. We migrate five Text-to-SQL benchmarks of different domains and source languages through a unified alignment pipeline that builds a shared cross-lingual entity map, migrates each database with its constraints, rewrites the gold SQL under execution-equivalence checks, and regenerates the question from the verified SQL, keeping all language versions of a task aligned in entities, intent, and execution result. MoL-SQL provides two splits: MoL-Full (16,892 samples) for overall evaluation and MoL-Cube (3,840 samples) covering every language combination to isolate question, schema, and value effects. Experiments with eight LLMs and four families of Text-to-SQL methods show that schema and value languages have distinct effects, with stored values costing up to 23 points, and that these effects mainly stem from cross-lingual entity mapping. Neither question translation nor additional reasoning narrows the value gap, whereas access to target-database values substantially reduces it, indicating that mixture-of-language Text-to-SQL benefits most from language-aware context grounding.

WeMM-Video: Pushing the Frontiers of Open-Source Omni-Modal Video Understanding#

Authors: Changli Tang, Jie Yang, Tianyi Wang, Lei Li, Daoze Zhang, Zijie Xin, Zhan Liu, Li Li, Jinchang Xu, Fengyun Rao, Chao Zhang, Jing Lyu Venue: arXiv (2026)

Video understanding, particularly with accompanying audio, remains a central challenge in multimodal intelligence. In this report, we introduce \textbf{WeMM-Video}, a family of Chinese-English bilingual omni-modal foundation models spanning 2B, 4B, 9B, and 35B-A3B variants. Centering on joint audio-visual perception and reasoning, WeMM-Video unifies text, image, audio, and video processing within a hybrid-thinking paradigm, seamlessly supporting both a non-thinking instruct mode for fast, direct responses and a thinking mode for explicit step-by-step reasoning. The models are trained on a large-scale multimodal corpus of approximately 1.2 trillion tokens through progressive pre-training and a multi-stage post-training pipeline incorporating multi-teacher OPD, anti-repetition MrDPO, and specialist RL. Evaluated across more than 120 benchmarks, WeMM-Video demonstrates superior performance in omni-modal video understanding and reasoning, while maintaining highly competitive capabilities across general audio, silent video, image, and text tasks. Specifically, in omni-modal video evaluations, our flagship WeMM-Video-35B-A3B establishes a new open-source state of the art, substantially outperforming proprietary models such as Qwen3.5-Omni-Flash and Seed2.0-Mini-0428 while narrowing the gap with the strong Gemini-3.0-Flash, and our compact WeMM-Video-9B surpasses the larger open-source Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Instruct. We publicly release the WeMM-Video family along with our evaluation and reproduction pipeline to foster further research and development in the open-source community.

PILAPS: A Bilingual Benchmark for Evaluating Pragmatic Inference across Phenomena and Settings#

Authors: Jiani Cao, Anna Korhonen, Chen Cecilia Liu Venue: arXiv (2026)

Recent work reports strong pragmatic performance by large language models (LLMs) in English, yet whether this performance generalises across languages and evaluation settings remains unclear. This question is increasingly important as LLMs are deployed across languages, cultures, and conversational contexts. In this paper, we first introduce PILAPS, a bilingual English/Mandarin Chinese benchmark covering eight pragmatic phenomena across purpose-written and dialogue-derived items, with both culturally adapted and independently sourced data. We then evaluate eight LLMs from four families across a range of model sizes and prompting strategies. Our results reveal substantial variation across models and pragmatic phenomena: open-source models continue to struggle particularly with humour, while cross-linguistic performance varies with model origin. Our error analysis shows that, compared with human participants, models are more prone to literal interpretations in conversational contexts. Theory-of-Mind prompting lowers the literal share of errors, whereas Chain-of-Thought prompting slightly raises it. Together, these findings demonstrate that strong pragmatic performance in one evaluation setting does not necessarily generalise across languages or conversational contexts.

An LLM-Agentic Framework for Intelligent Work Order Management in Industrial Maintenance#

Authors: Nianjun Zhou, Dhaval Patel Venue: arXiv (2026)

This paper presents an AI-driven framework, CodeReAct agent, designed to enhance industrial equipment maintenance through natural language (NL) interactions. The framework employs an advanced AI agentic approach to interpret human instructions, providing knowledge extraction, asset status summarization, and work order recommendations. It identifies patterns and trends and predicts work orders by standardizing industrial data into business objects (BOs) and utilizing analytical function API calls. The AI agent operates through a Thought-Action-Observation process similar to human problem-solving, automating complex analytical tasks typically performed by data scientists. Notably, the Action phase generates executable code, replacing manual coding efforts. A case study focusing on chiller maintenance demonstrates the framework’s capability to analyze multiple years of work orders, and alerts and anomalies are presented. The results indicate that the AI agent framework effectively processes human utterances to analyze data, extract valuable insights, and support informed decision-making, reducing the need for data scientist involvement. By recontextualizing NLP agent frameworks in industrial engineering, the approach enables autonomous function generation and task execution across a range of domain-specific maintenance scenarios, with chillers used as a representative case study.

Can Programs Replace LLM Judges? Executable Program Judges for Scalable and Efficient Relevance Labeling#

Authors: Eugene Agichtein, Charles L. A. Clarke, Kaustubh Dhole, William Stern Venue: arXiv (2026)

Large language model (LLM) judges have become a common way to create large-scale relevance judgments for retrieval evaluation. However, each judgment requires a potentially expensive call to a frontier model, and the judgments are hard to interpret and may not be reproducible. In this paper, we ask whether an LLM can write a readable program for each topic that replaces its per-document judgments. To address these issues, we propose ExecJudges, a method for generating fast, inspectable and scalable judges for automatic relevance labeling. Specifically, ExecJudges uses a frontier LLM to turn assessment guidelines into executable programs, which rely only on lexical matching, standard NLP libraries like parsers, and pool statistics. An ExecJudges-L variant also uses examples of human labels if available. We evaluate ExecJudges on seven TREC collections by comparing the produced labels with trained NIST assessors, frontier LLM judges, and recently proposed per-topic classifiers. Our results show that ExecJudges agrees with NIST assessors at the level of the Claude Sonnet 5.5 LLM judges on TREC DL 2019-2021. With a small number of human labels per topic, the ExecJudges-L variant agrees with NIST more than per-topic classifiers with the same labels on all seven collections, and either exceeds the agreement of LLM judges provided the same labels or is within about 0.05 of it in Krippendorff’s alpha. The labels rank the official TREC DL runs with Kendall’s tau of 0.87 to 0.94, and writing the programs incurs a one-time cost of $0.17 to $0.49 per topic, with no additional costs for grading. Our results suggest that ExecJudges offers a reproducible, inspectable, efficient and cost-effective alternative to LLM judges for labeling deep result pools for system evaluation or ranking improvements.

Compiling Constraints into Neuromorphic Ising Machines#

Authors: Faiek Ahsan, Shantanu Chakrabartty Venue: arXiv (2026)

Most practical combinatorial optimization problems involve constraints, and an Ising machine that flips one spin at a time can respect them only through energy penalties that raise barriers between feasible solutions. Here we show that a higher-order neuromorphic Ising machine can enforce constraints through programmable transitions rather than penalties. In an autoencoder-like architecture, each inner-layer neuron represents a move, a family of multi-spin transitions that maps every feasible state to another feasible state, receives the energy change of its move through connections that outer-layer neurons multiplicatively switch on or off, and updates the state through the same connections. A compiler turns constraints into this wiring with a number of auxiliary clauses that grows only polynomially for parity, cardinality, one-hot, partition and permutation constraints. Restricting the search to feasible states reduces constraint handling to move design, and we show that the fastest-annealing moves are not those that mix fastest but those that lower the deepest energy barriers, and that moves chosen from the objective lower them further than random ones. On third-order graph matching, the machine needs orders of magnitude fewer iterations than penalty-based neuromorphic machines. Configured as a sampler on a field-programmable gate array, it decodes the surface code with maximum-likelihood accuracy up to code distance 15 within 52 $μ$s per shot and, under noisy syndrome measurements, decodes in real time for millisecond measurement rounds up to distance 9 with fewer logical errors than belief-propagation decoders.

A Neurosymbolic Multimodal Framework for 30-Day Diabetic Readmission Prediction: Integrating Structured EHR Data, Dense Clinical Embeddings, and Knowledge-Grounded Symbolic Extraction#

Authors: Una Suman Kumar Patro, Sibangi Subhadarsani, Narendra Badam Venue: arXiv (2026)

Unplanned 30-day readmissions among patients with diabetes mellitus impose a substantial clinical and economic burden, yet most predictive models use only structured electronic health record (EHR) data and ignore free-text clinical notes, which often contain code-mixed Hindi-English (Hinglish) phrasing and negation that general-purpose language models parse unreliably. We propose a neurosymbolic multimodal framework evaluated on the public Diabetes 130-US Hospitals dataset (101,766 encounters; 71,518 patients) with a patient-level grouped 80/20 split. Three streams are fused: engineered structured features; 32 components of Bio_ClinicalBERT note embeddings obtained by truncated singular value decomposition; and symbolic entities extracted by an open-weight clinical language model within a three-stage hybrid retrieval-augmented generation pipeline (dense retrieval, sparse lexical retrieval, and cross-encoder re-ranking) with reflective negation auditing. A cost-sensitive XGBoost classifier with a decision threshold calibrated by Youden’s J statistic produces the prediction. On the held-out test cohort (n = 20,153), the structured baseline achieved an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) of 0.6477, sensitivity of 0.5839, and Brier score of 0.1080. The full multimodal model achieved the highest sensitivity (0.6667; +8.28 percentage points), the best F2-score (0.3824), and the lowest Brier score (0.0977), at a slightly lower AUROC (0.6375) and specificity (0.5161). Shapley additive explanations showed that prior utilization dominates risk, with compressed narrative components among the top predictors. On a 500-note code-switched benchmark, grounded retrieval raised Hinglish symptom recall from 4.8% to 40.2% and precision from 3.1% to 45.2%, while reducing entity hallucinations from 1.74 to 0.00 per note.

Bias-Only Training Dynamics in ReLU Networks#

Authors: Shreyas Chaudhari, Jose M. F. Moura Venue: arXiv (2026)

Neural networks with randomly initialized, fixed weights and trainable biases are universal approximators, yet the dynamics of bias-only training remain largely unexplored. We study these dynamics in one-hidden-layer ReLU networks through activation codes, the binary patterns of active neurons that partition the input space into convex polyhedral regions. We show that the gradients with respect to the biases depend on each input only through its activation code, and hence the training dynamics can be characterized with these codes. In particular, as long as the activation codes remain fixed, training reduces to a linear least-squares problem with the codes as features. Training can then be understood as a sequence of least-squares problems, with transitions occurring when neurons change their activation states. We show that the fraction of switching neurons vanishes as the network widens, and the training dynamics approach those of linear least squares on the initial activation codes in the infinite-width limit. Our experiments support these theoretical findings, showing that while activation switches can noticeably affect training in narrower networks, their relative frequency and the associated changes in the dynamics diminish as the width increases.

Separating QMA from the QCMA Hierarchy via a Switching Lemma for Polynomials#

Authors: Yiming Wang Venue: arXiv (2026)

We construct a classical oracle relative to which QMA is not contained in the QCMA hierarchy. Higher-Scoring Coding Models Did Not Get More Work Done#

Authors: Yining Hua Venue: arXiv (2026)

New coding models advertise themselves on score improvements of coding benchmarks. Yet no study has tested if a model that scores higher on these benchmarks gets more real work done. On June 12, 2026, a U.S. export control directive banned access to Claude Fable 5 for 19 days, which forced users to switch to other models. During this time, most former Fable 5 users switched to Opus 4.8 (11 points lower on SWE-Bench Pro compared to Fable 5). We treat this suspension as a natural experiment, where the people and projects stayed the same and only the model changed, so we could isolate the effect of Fable 5 on user productivity. We tracked GitHub pull requests of 297 Fable 5 users before, during, and after the suspension. We used 8,908 developers who stayed on Opus 4.8 all the time as the control group. We also repeated the comparison across five model upgrades before and after the suspension in 2026 (1,208 developers, 15,442 pull requests). Fitting a difference-in-differences model with developer and date fixed effects, we found that having access to Fable 5 increased the 7-day merge rate of pull requests by only 1.4 percentage points [-1.4; +4.3], which was not statistically significant. Similarly, no significant increases of merge rates were found with the upgrades (weighted mean: -0.9 percentage points [-1.9; +0.2]); all five estimates were negative (95% intervals included 0, did not differ from each other). We also did not observe a significant increase in the number of pull requests merged per week (+2.4% [-4.9%; +10.2%]).

Magic state cultivation with lattice-surgery escape on superconducting hardware#

Authors: Haoran Liao, Michael Schutzman, William Munizzi, Pranav Mundada, Gavin Hartnett, Yuval Baum Venue: arXiv (2026)

Magic state cultivation (MSC) is a low-overhead protocol for fault-tolerantly preparing logical magic states. In this work, we implement two MSC variants to prepare a magic state in a distance-5 code on a publicly available IBM Nighthawk processor. The first is a lattice-surgery escape, recently proposed by Hirano et al. and experimentally studied here for the first time. The second is a grafting escape, previously demonstrated by Rosenfeld et al., and extended here by incorporating a switch into a matchable code. Both protocols begin with fault-tolerant cultivation of a T state on a color code, followed by an escape into a larger code. We decode the results using minimum-weight perfect matching or Tesseract on circuit-level error models calibrated to the hardware noise. Compared with the previous experiment, our cultivated T state achieves a logical error probability of 2e-5 at the T-state injection angle, nearly 10x lower with about 3x the retainment. Moreover, our execution of the grafting escape has a 2x lower logical error rate per stabilizer round at a higher retainment. When comparing the two protocols under identical execution conditions and complementary gap postselection based on decoder confidence, lattice-surgery escape into the surface code preserves a T-state fidelity above 0.99 ten rounds after escape at about 5% retainment. By contrast, matchable-switched grafting drops below 0.95 fidelity at comparable retainment. Furthermore, the lattice-surgery escape reaches a logical error rate per stabilizer round of about 0.14% with around 11% average shot retainment. These results establish lattice-surgery escape as a practical MSC protocol that benefits from a direct transition into a surface code, and demonstrate the lowest MSC error and the highest surface-code magic-state fidelity reported to date, advancing the experimental realization of fault-tolerant non-Clifford gadgets.

HALTT: A HAte Lexicon Through Time#

Authors: Dario Franzese, Farah Benamara, Véronique Moriceau, Viviana Patti Venue: arXiv (2026)

Automated hate speech detection degrades over time as everyday vocabulary is weaponized into covert slurs while retaining original benign meanings (polysemy retention).

Label-Wording Augmentation in a Typed-Decision Encoder: Gains on Unfamiliar Words, Not on Abbreviations or Codes#

Authors: Vladimir Beskorovainyi Venue: arXiv (2026)

Typed-decision models score options that the caller supplies at request time, so their accuracy depends on label wording that the model’s developer does not control. We compare two releases of a 307M-parameter multilingual cross-encoder, Ru-Jev, trained on thirteen Russian-language tasks; the later release adds an augmentation that presents labels in several surface forms. Holding questions fixed and varying only how eighteen routing labels are worded, we evaluate ten label vocabularies, produced by three separate processes and checked for overlap with the training data, with five candidates per question and, for eight of them, also with eighteen. On held-out vocabularies made of ordinary or borrowed words (English-derived jargon, office slang, department names), the augmented release is more accurate in every setting, by up to 0.24. Eleven of these fourteen gains survive a Holm correction (slang with eighteen candidates and descriptive sentences with five do not), and for jargon on all labels the label-level interval excludes zero in both settings. On abbreviations and opaque codes it gives no usable gain: on abbreviations it is not better, and worse on the clean slice, and on opaque codes both releases stay at or below chance once labels related to training options are removed. In most cells where accuracy rises, the risk-coverage area falls, but with eighteen candidates confident errors rise on jargon and slang. On two datasets neither release saw, accuracy moves by -8.4 and +5.6 points. We also report four evaluation hazards from comparing the two releases.

Cultural Adaptation within Repeated Social Interactions#

Authors: Chen Cecilia Liu, Roi Reichart, Anna Korhonen Venue: arXiv (2026)

Much of human culture is tacit and is typically learned through situated social interaction. As LLMs are deployed as interactive agents across diverse communities, assessing their cultural competence demands moving beyond existing question-answering approaches, shifting from what they know to how they adapt. In this work, we broaden the scope of cultural adaptation in NLP by investigating whether LLMs can adapt through repeated interactions via a sandbox environment. By reframing cultural adaptation as a continuous process of norm assimilation through social interaction, our work demonstrates that memory mechanisms foster longitudinal learning across repeated interactions, facilitate transfer to different social settings, and enable multilingual norm assimilation across five languages. Together, our findings lay the groundwork for continuous, interaction-driven cultural adaptation in LLMs.

Polarity Classification with Multitask Learning Syntax Augmentation#

Authors: Muhammad Imran, Carlos Gómez-Rodríguez Venue: arXiv (2026)

Sentiment Analysis (SA) is a fundamental NLP task concerned with the identification and categorization of subjective information in text. While lexicon-based methods offer transparency, they often struggle with linguistic phenomena such as negation and intensification. Syntactic parsing has been integrated into SA pipelines to address this, but conventional parsers introduce significant computational overhead. Recent work shows that reformulating dependency parsing as a Sequence Labeling (SEL) task can improve efficiency. We propose and compare two strategies for integrating syntactic knowledge into a neural sentiment analysis model under unified Multi-Task Learning (MTL). The first, Standard-MTL, jointly learns dependency parsing (via sequence labeling) and sentiment polarity classification using a shared encoder with two task-specific heads. The second, Contrastive-MTL, provides implicit syntactic guidance through a contrastive auxiliary objective and a syntax-biased attention pooling mechanism, without requiring explicit label prediction. Both rely on syntactic annotations during training and eliminate need for hand-crafted compositional rules or external parsing at inference. We evaluate eleven datasets spanning seven languages—the MARC corpus (English, Spanish, French, German) and seven datasets from SemEval-2022 Task 10 (English, Spanish, Norwegian, Basque, Catalan)—using four established SEL encodings. With the relative encoding, Contrastive-MTL outperforms a syntax-naive baseline on 10 of 11 datasets and surpasses the external rule-based pipeline (SELSP) that parses at inference on all six datasets where the latter applies, while being 1.5–9$\times$ faster at evaluation. Standard-MTL is more dataset- and encoding-dependent. These results show implicit syntactic supervision during training can match or exceed explicit parsing at inference without computational overhead.

Knowing When to Teach: Learning from Teachable Prefixes in Multilingual Reasoning Distillation#

Authors: Jaewon Cho, Minseo Yoon, Jinyoung Park, Jinwoo Seo, Jaewon Chu, Alice Oh, Hyunwoo J. Kim Venue: arXiv (2026)

On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) improves reasoning by training a student on its own reasoning, using the same model conditioned on reference solutions as a teacher. Applying OPSD to multilingual reasoning requires the teacher to provide useful feedback across languages, even when the student makes mistakes in intermediate steps. We observe a phenomenon we call Teacher Drift: as these mistakes accumulate, the teacher may follow the student’s incorrect steps instead of correcting them. Our analysis shows that this shift tends to occur earlier in lower-resource languages. To account for these differences across languages, we propose Teachable On-Policy Self-Distillation (TOPSD), which determines where and whether teacher supervision should be applied to each reasoning trajectory. TOPSD uses teacher-student likelihood to estimate where drift begins and select a prefix for supervision. It then verifies whether the teacher can recover the correct answer from that prefix, applying distillation only to prefixes that pass verification. Experiments on multilingual mathematical reasoning across ten languages and three model scales show that TOPSD consistently outperforms conventional OPSD, particularly in low- and medium-resource languages.

Circe: Training-Free LLM Self-Evaluation via Component Interaction Consistency#

Authors: Shuyi Miao, Zheng Li, Xu Xie, Linrui Xu, Fei Shen, Xiaoyu Du, Tat-Seng Chua Venue: arXiv (2026)

As large language models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed in real-world applications, evaluating the correctness of their responses is crucial for reliable use. However, such evaluation is challenging because reference answers are rarely available in practice. Existing reference-free methods mainly rely on output signals, which may assign high scores to incorrect responses and thus do not necessarily reflect correctness. This motivates exploring the model’s internal computation for complementary correctness signals. Recent studies exploit internal states, but they mainly analyze hidden-state trajectories across layers, which record only the combined updates of attention and feed-forward networks (FFNs) and overlook how these components interact. We therefore propose Circe, a training-free self-evaluation method that assesses a response using both internal and external signals, without reference answers or repeated sampling. For internal signals, we first introduce component interaction consistency (CIC), which measures the directional alignment between attention updates in earlier layers and attention-induced FFN output changes in later layers. We further incorporate representation geometry (RG) to characterize normalized displacement and angular changes along layerwise hidden-state trajectories. For the external signal, we convert the mean negative log-likelihood of generated tokens into a bounded likelihood confidence (LC) score. Finally, we fuse CIC, RG, and LC into a unified correctness score. Experiments on five LLMs and six benchmarks across four domains show that our method distinguishes correct from incorrect responses more effectively than existing baselines, and generalizes to multilingual, multimodal, and open-world settings.

CardioLM: A multilingual suite of small language models for the cardiology domain#

Authors: Manuela-Daniela Danu, Miguel Rodríguez-Ortega, Alberto Becerra-Tome, Pablo Arancibia, Fernando Gallego-Donoso, Lorenzo Pratesi, Xi Bai, Andrei-Damian Dinescu, Salvador Lima-López, Andrea Guala, Saskia Haitjema, Jonas Mack, Michele Miranda, Wesam Al Nabki, Jan Rodríguez-Miret, Vasile George Marica, Vladimír Kincl, Kateřina Dostálová, Michael Andrej, Alicia Ujil, Corinne Allaart, Machteld Boonstra, Malin Overmars, Anoop Shah, Giovanna Ceroni, Jonathan Kossoff, Domenico D’Amario, Renzo Laborante, Antonio Iaconelli, Isabella Fumarulo, Jacopo Lenkowicz, Livia Lilli, Emma Weiss, Alex Scafa, Felix Lindberg, Milou Gamage, Eulàlia Farré-Maduell, Sébastien Bratières, Martin Krallinger, Bram van Es Venue: arXiv (2026)

Background: Cardiovascular care generates large volumes of clinical free-text documentation in local languages. However, clinical natural language processing (NLP) resources remain concentrated in English, and no existing benchmark is cardiology-specific, multilingual, and broad in entity coverage. Methods: We present two contributions. The first is CardioCCC, a corpus of 508 cardiology case reports originally published in Spanish and extended to Czech, Dutch, English, Italian, Romanian, and Swedish through machine translation, annotation projection, and clinician review, with annotations covering diseases, symptoms, procedures, and medications. The second is CardioLM, comprising CardioBERTa, a set of language-specific encoders obtained by continued pretraining on cardiology and biomedical corpora, and CardioNER, a set of named entity recognition (NER) models fine-tuned on CardioCCC. Results: Cardiology-specific pretraining reduced pseudo-perplexity by up to a factor of eight, yet changed strict span-level F1 by at most 0.04, with benefits concentrated in languages whose backbones lacked biomedical coverage. The jointly trained multilingual XLM-RoBERTa-large models achieved the best strict F1 in nearly every language and entity type, exceeding the strongest monolingual system by up to 0.13 F1. A large share of residual errors concerned boundary detection: relaxed matching recovered 0.10-0.23 F1 for disease, procedure, and symptom, but only 0.01-0.07 for medication. Clinicians rated CardioNER well above a rule- and dictionary-based baseline, with the mean rating across entity types rising from 26% to 73%. Conclusions: Specialty-specific pretraining is not the decisive factor for downstream entity recognition, whereas multilingual joint training yields the strongest results in nearly all settings and a simpler deployment path for multi-country clinical information extraction.

SAUCE: Summary Analysis Using Conversation Entailment#

Authors: Man-Ling Sung, Hemanth Kandula, Jeff Ma, William Hartmann, Matthew Snover Venue: arXiv (2026)

With the growing need for evaluating Large Language Models (LLMs) and their applications to speech, challenges persist in summarizing and evaluating conversations that lack a clear end goal. We introduce SAUCE – a reference-free, fact-based evaluation pipeline for cross-lingual conversational speech summarization. It measures the accuracy and the fact coverage of a summary through the entailment between conversation and text. We compare SAUCE against several popular summarization metrics and demonstrate the effectiveness of capturing information loss due to transcription and translation error and identifying broken summaries. Crucially, unlike black-box LLM evaluators or dense embedding metrics, SAUCE is inherently explainable: it maps summary scores to discrete, verifiable facts, allowing users to pinpoint exact hallucinations or omissions. We illustrate how this interpretability helps developers systematically profile LLM behaviors and gives end-users an actionable tool to verify summary accuracy in noisy, real-world conditions. Preliminary investigations show SAUCE strongly align with human judgment.

CardioVascBench:Prioritized Diagnosis Generation from Real-World Chinese Cardiovascular Records#

Authors: Minghao Kong, Muyao Chen, Jianhao Wu, Yuqiang Zhang, Xiangbin Meng, Zirong Liu, Ting Cao, Kaida Ning, Zhen Song, Rongjie Wang Venue: arXiv (2026)

Identifying the primary diagnosis alone does not fully characterize a model’s diagnosis-generation performance. We introduce CardioVascBench, a retrospective benchmark for prioritized diagnosis generation from real-world Chinese cardiovascular inpatient records. It contains 3,262 de-identified cases, with 2,174 for training and 1,088 for evaluation, linking clinical narratives and textual ECG reports to expert-verified, ordered discharge diagnoses standardized to ICD-10. An English translation of the evaluation set supports aligned cross-lingual comparison. Our evaluation protocol combines terminology normalization and rank-aware scoring to separately assess primary-diagnosis identification, reference-diagnosis coverage, and list precision. Evaluation across 36 systems shows that higher primary-diagnosis accuracy does not necessarily imply higher list precision against discharge references. We also provide CardioKG-Agent, a knowledge-enhanced baseline combining entity normalization, constrained graph retrieval, and guideline evidence. In a staged comparison of three baseline models, supervised fine-tuning plus the agent achieves a 73.5% higher mean composite score than fine-tuning alone, representing a system-level gain. These results measure retrospective agreement with recorded diagnoses; agreement between metric rankings and physician judgments remains to be validated. CardioVascBench supports research on diagnosis prioritization and knowledge-enhanced generation.

TTSonic: A Unified Framework for Composable and Controllable Speech Generation#

Authors: Zhedong Zhang, Kaiwen Zhou, Junyan Wu, Shengqiang Li, Xuran Chi, Xingui Zeng, Rui Yan, Xu Chen Venue: arXiv (2026)

Modern text-to-speech (TTS) systems can synthesize natural and expressive speech, yet a practical speech foundation model must go beyond naturalness to offer stable quality, precise controllability, and a broad set of composable capabilities within a unified framework. We present TTSonic, a unified speech generation model that supports zero-shot TTS, voice cloning, prosody cloning, instruction following, voice conversion, and cloning from noisy reference speech. Rather than relying on separate task-specific models, TTSonic accommodates diverse conditioning signals, including text, natural language instructions, reference speech, and text-described timbre, within the same model framework. Specifically, we develop a semantic–acoustic disentangled speech tokenizer through ASR-supervised semantic learning and reconstruction-guided generative refinement. Serving as the representational foundation of our unified framework, the tokenizer enables the Text-to-Semantic autoregressive model to focus on semantic and prosodic planning, while the acoustic flow-matching model specializes in high-quality speech rendering under diverse acoustic and timbre conditions. Building on this framework, we incorporate CoT-guided instruction-following planning and Text2Timbre voice design to support controllable speech generation. Experiments across zero-shot synthesis, multilingual and cross-lingual TTS, instruction following, voice design, voice conversion, and noisy-reference cloning demonstrate the competitive performance and broad applicability of TTSonic.

Steering with Multilingual Lexicons for Sentiment Control in Low-Resource Languages#

Authors: Lucinda Laurent, Muhammad Dehan Al Kautsar, Alfan Farizki Wicaksono, Fajri Koto Venue: arXiv (2026)

Activation steering offers a lightweight mechanism for controlling model behavior, but existing methods depend on sentence-level contrastive data that is unavailable for most of the world’s languages. We show that multilingual sentiment lexicons are a viable substitute across three model families: aggregated multilingual lexicons outperform English-derived steering vectors for mid- and low-resource languages, while monolingual lexicons remain stronger for languages a model has specialized in during pre-training. The steering efficacy is mechanistically dictated by both representational density and pre-training priors. Large-magnitude English vectors overwhelm the compact representations of low-resource languages, causing “linguistic collapse.” This fragility is mitigated by the norm-dampening of multilingual aggregation. Architectural biases, including bilingual specialization penalty and latent numerical rigidity, also heavily constrain cross-lingual steering responsiveness. Ultimately, our work establishes lexicons as a practical steering signal and offers a comprehensive mechanistic account of cross-lingual steering dynamics.

MultiInpaintCOCO: Fine-Grained Visual Understanding in Multiple Languages#

Authors: Andrei-Alexandru Manea, Jindřich Libovický, Philipp J. Rösch Venue: arXiv (2026)

Previous research shows that vision-language (VL) models struggle to understand fine-grained image details and tend to focus on the overall layout. Moreover, any weakness in English tends to be further amplified in the multilingual setting. To study that, we extend InpaintCOCO, an English dataset focused on fine-grained visual details, to 13 typologically diverse languages across five language families, creating MultiInpaintCOCO - a new multilingual benchmark of 953 minimal-pair examples. We further test several state-of-the-art models in multilingual multimodal retrieval, considering both light VL CLIP-like encoders and large language models. Our results show that performance degrades substantially for lower-resource languages, with the strongest predictor being the data resources available for the languages rather than typological similarity to English. In cross-lingual retrieval, most models exhibit a systematic bias toward English: when given a correct caption in a non-English language and an incorrect one in English, models preferentially select the English caption. This bias varies considerably across architectures. Among the tested models, Gemma 3 shows the strongest English preference while Qwen 3 is the least biased, despite both reaching similar monolingual accuracy across languages. The code is available at https://github.com/ufal/multi-inpaint-coco.

PlagiaCorpus: A Bilingual, Adversarial, and Cross-Lingual Dataset for Forensic Plagiarism Detection#

Authors: Adel Ammar, Serry Sibaee, Wadii Boulila Venue: arXiv (2026)

PlagiaCorpus is a bilingual Arabic-English dataset for benchmarking plagiarism and text-reuse detection, including reuse produced by large language models (LLMs) and reuse across languages. It contains 198,643 text records: a source corpus of 34,885 passages drawn from English and Arabic Wikipedia and two openly licensed book collections, and 163,758 queries (147,381 for training and 16,377 for testing). The queries cover five reuse classes of increasing difficulty, namely verbatim copying, character-level obfuscation, same-language paraphrasing by two different LLMs, and English–Arabic translation with structural paraphrasing, together with a Safe class of non-reused passages. Every positive query is linked to its source passage, and the train/test split is grouped by source passage so that no passage contributes queries to both splits. The release was curated through language identification, removal of LLM generation artefacts, and exact and cross-lingual de-duplication. Technical validation reports per-class length statistics, automatic checks of the train/test split, and inter-annotator agreement on a manually rated sample. The dataset is distributed in JSON and Parquet formats under a CC BY-SA 4.0 license.

STARE: Scene Text-Aware multimodal Retrieval for Everyday objects#

Authors: Kento Tokura, Ryosuke Korekata, Daichi Yashima, Kei Katsumata, Kanon Amemiya, Miyu Goko, Komei Sugiura Venue: arXiv (2026)

In this study, we focus on service robots that retrieve and transport everyday objects based on free-form instructions that may refer to visual features and scene text. This is challenging because it requires a multimodal understanding of the visual features and spatial relationships of objects, as well as scene text embedded in the environment. To address this, we propose Scene Text-Aware multimodal Retrieval for Everyday objects (STARE), which leverages visual and textual cues in response to free-form instructions. STARE introduces Crosslingual Visual Prompt to enhance scene text interpretation, and models the complex relationships between named entities in the instructions and their corresponding objects. We evaluate STARE using two novel benchmarks, GoGetIt and TextCaps-test, both of which consist of real-world indoor/outdoor images and instructions involving object manipulation and navigation. The results show that STARE outperforms all baseline methods on standard multimodal retrieval metrics. Furthermore, in physical experiments conducted under a zero-shot transfer setting, STARE achieves an overall success rate of 80%. Our project page and code are provided in the Supplementary Materials.

VoxCanvas-41M: A Large-Scale Bilingual Dataset for Instruction-Based Speech Editing and Its Learning Dynamics#

Authors: Zhikang Niu, Wenming Tu, Yalin Li, Yanru Huo, Junxi Liu, Tianrui Wang, Ruiqi Yan, Junxuan Liang, Ziyang Ma, Zilong Zheng, Xie Chen Venue: arXiv (2026)

Recent advances in speech generation have spurred the development of speech editing techniques. However, achieving high-quality speech editing remains challenging due to two key limitations. First, the major hurdle is the lack of large-scale, high-quality speech editing datasets. Constructing such datasets is costly, while open-source, scalable data pipelines remain limited. Second, the learning dynamics of speech editing models remain poorly understood. In particular, how models acquire editing capabilities and the relationship between generation and editing remain unclear. To address these challenges, we introduce VoxCanvas-41M, a large-scale bilingual dataset comprising approximately 41 million speech editing pairs. VoxCanvas-41M covers two major categories: content editing, including single-span, multi-span, and location-reference editing, and paralinguistic editing, covering the insertion and deletion of 39 classes of nonverbal events such as laughter, sighs, and coughs. Building on VoxCanvas-41M, we train VoxCanvas-Edit, an instruction-based speech editing model, demonstrating the effectiveness of our dataset in improving editing performance. Furthermore, we systematically investigate the learning dynamics of speech editing, examining how models learn different editing operations and how data scale, task interactions, and training strategies affect editing performance. The dataset, pipeline, and demos are publicly available.

From Twisted Facts to Rogue Agents: Can LLMs Reliably Detect Multi-Dimensional Hallucinations?#

Authors: Hanyu Qiu, Ze Liu, Zhe Zhao, Kuang Xiong, Blaze Chen, Zhenxiang Yan, Renfen Hu Venue: arXiv (2026)

Large language models (LLMs) are widely used for information access, content production, and agent-based task execution, but their outputs remain vulnerable to subtle, high-risk hallucinations. To systematically evaluate and advance post-generation hallucination detection, we introduce HaluCheck, a large-scale, bilingual benchmark comprising over 42k instances spanning both factual hallucinations in text and faithfulness hallucinations in agent trajectories. HaluCheck features fine-grained, multi-level annotations for detection, localization, and correction, covering different tasks and perturbation degrees. Experiments on fourteen LLMs reveal that, while models show basic sample-level detection ability, they struggle with fine-grained localization, correction, and agent hallucination detection. Further analysis demonstrates that thinking, web search, and supervised fine-tuning each offer partial improvements, but none fully solve the problem. HaluCheck thus provides a solid basis for benchmarking and diagnosing LLM reliability across text and agent scenarios.

VocabulARy 2.0: A Generative AR System Enabling Personalised 3D Mnemonics for Vocabulary Learning in Context#

Authors: Karolina Trajkovska, Matjaž Kljun, Maheshya Weerasinghe, Klen Čopič Pucihar Venue: arXiv (2026)

Mnemonics support vocabulary learning by linking unfamiliar words to familiar concepts through memorable verbal and visual associations. We present a generative AR system that enables learners to create personalised 3D mnemonics by transforming familiar objects in their surroundings into situated mnemonic anchors. The system scans the environment, detects visible objects, and generates bilingual flashcards. After selecting a word, the learner describes a personal association. The system combines this description with an image of the selected object to generate a textured 3D model using generative AI. Learners can then place the model relative to the real object, transforming it into a situated mnemonic anchor.

Airlock: Bounding the Multi-Agent Blast Radius via Selective Online Containment#

Authors: Zhonghao Zhan, Javad Forough, Yefan Zhang, Krinos Li, Xiao Ma, Hamed Haddadi Venue: arXiv (2026)

When multi-agent systems act on the external world, a single agent’s faulty output can spread across tasks before detection, creating a system-wide blast radius. Single-agent guardrails are confined to individual harnesses, while existing multi-agent controls either cancel only the faulty source and miss downstream consumers or halt and roll back the entire workflow at the cost of healthy work. We present Airlock, a multi-agent runtime and guardrail for selective post-alert fault containment. Its tree-structured runtime, EdgeCitadel, narrows permissions vertically from parent to child agents to establish node boundaries and control switches. Meanwhile, an append-only provenance DAG records cross-agent data consumption horizontally. Upon an alert, Airlock traces forward on this DAG to isolate tainted executions and pending actions, and backward to narrow root causes.
